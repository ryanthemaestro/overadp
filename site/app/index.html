OverADP — How to Use the War Room

OverADP is an AI-powered fantasy football draft assistant. It uses machine learning trained on historical NFL data plus current roster overlays, teammate target competition, draft-value rookie signals, prior-year contract signals, and split-conformal quantile regression for 80%-target projection ranges. Target Intel uses opportunity-cost draft math that adapts to roster settings, live picks, VBD, ADP, flex paths, and player availability before your next turn. The projection model and daily draft-market refresh are dated separately so you can verify freshness.

Draft Board Sort

Offensive players (QB/RB/WR/TE) are sorted by an opportunity-cost draft score. VBD and projections measure value, ADP measures price, and the board estimates whether a player is likely to make it back to your next pick. Kickers (K) and Defenses (DEF) are sorted by projected points since they have no meaningful VBD signal.

My Team (Center Panel)

Roster slots are driven by your league configuration (Settings). Players are auto-assigned in priority order:

  • QB → QB slot(s), then Superflex (if configured)
  • RB → RB1, RB2 (or however many RB slots), then Flex, then Bench
  • WR → WR slots, then Flex, then Bench
  • TE → TE slot(s), then Flex, then Bench
  • K / DEF → their dedicated slots (configurable count)

Bye weeks are shown next to each player. Red = bye conflict with another starter at the same position.

Key Concepts

VBD (Value Based Drafting)

VBD = Projected Points − Replacement Level Points. The replacement level is the projected points of the last player typically drafted at each position (including bench). This means even bench-worthy players get positive VBD, helping you compare value across all rounds — not just the early ones.

Target Intel

Target Intel is separate from the point-projection model. It asks a draft-specific question: given your roster, league settings, current pick, next pick, ADP price, and the players still available, who is worth taking now?

The recommendation layer is ADP-guarded: ADP remains the price anchor, but the app boosts players with enough VBD, roster need, and scarcity to justify taking them now, especially when the next-turn estimate says they probably will not make it back.

Roster settings matter. No-flex, multi-flex, 2QB, unusual starter counts, and very shallow or deep benches all change the opportunity cost and next-pick math directly.

Positional Scarcity

Measures the drop-off from the elite tier to replacement level at each position. If QB has a small dropoff but TE has a huge one, you should prioritize TE — because the quality falls off a cliff after the top TEs are gone.

How it's calculated: For each position, OverADP finds the projected points of the #1 player (elite) and the projected points of the last starter that would be rostered across all teams (replacement). The difference is the scarcity score.

Scarcity LevelWhat It MeansDraft Strategy
High ScarcityHuge gap between elite and replacementDraft this position early — waiting means getting a much worse player
Medium ScarcityModerate dropoffBalanced — draft when value aligns with your pick
Low ScarcityShallow dropoff — many comparable optionsSafe to wait — replacement-level players are almost as good as "starters"

Example: If the gap from the top available tight end to a replacement-level tight end is much larger than the comparable quarterback gap, tight end is more scarce for your league settings. The live scarcity panel calculates those gaps from the current projections and your roster configuration, so the example never depends on stale player names or point totals.

Projection Range (CQR)

The 80% target range is built via Conformalized Quantile Regression — separate 10th- and 90th-percentile models plus a split-conformal adjustment learned from 2025 calibration rows.

The procedure targets 80% marginal coverage across comparable players. Its post-adjustment 2025 coverage is a calibration diagnostic, not an independent test result or a guarantee for one player.

Rookies and 2nd-year players get a 1.8× / 1.3× width multiplier to reflect their scarcer historical data.

Handcuff Alerts

A handcuff is a backup RB on the same NFL team as your starting RB. If your starter gets injured, their backup typically takes over the starting role and inherits most of the carries — making them extremely valuable as insurance.

Why it matters: If you drafted Saquon Barkley in the 1st round and he goes down in Week 3, his backup (who you can draft in the last round for almost nothing) suddenly becomes a top-15 RB. Without the handcuff, you're scrambling on waivers.

Handcuff TypeDescriptionPriority
Must HandcuffYour RB1's backup — protect your 1st/2nd round investmentHigh — draft in late rounds
ConsiderYour RB2/RB3's backup — nice insurance but less criticalMedium — if bench space allows
AvailableOther backup RBs still on the boardLow — only if you have empty bench slots

Strategy: Draft your stud RB's handcuff with one of your last picks. It's like buying insurance — you hope you never need it, but if you do, it saves your season. The Handcuff Alerts panel shows you which backups are still available for RBs already on your roster.

Model Architecture

OverADP currently runs tuned CatBoost models per position, selected after walk-forward experiments against simpler baselines. The production models use per-position feature sets, temporal sample weighting (more recent seasons matter more), and leakage-controlled lag features so current-season outcomes never feed their own predictions.

80%-target projection ranges come from a parallel split-conformal quantile regression pipeline (see Projection Range section above).

ModelRole
CatBoost (production)Ordered boosting for the QB/RB/WR/TE projection models.
Target IntelOpportunity-cost next-pick scoring for live draft recommendations.
ADP BaselineHeld out as the market comparison, not used as a target leak.
Conformal QuantilesParallel interval pipeline for 80%-target projection ranges.
K/DEFCurrent depth-chart starters ranked for Weeks 1–3 by market-implied scoring, opponent, venue, and a backtested late-round streaming policy.

Key Features (August 2026)

  • Current roster overlay — Sleeper roster/depth data keeps teams, bye weeks, and post-draft movement aligned with the board.
  • Current availability overlay — injuries, reserve-list designations, practice status, expected returns, and suspensions are refreshed throughout draft season.
  • Target / carry competition — Sum of teammate prior-year production on current roster. Captures FA moves and scheme shifts directly.
  • College & draft pipeline — Draft capital, draft-pick value, combine athleticism, college per-game production, Power 5 flags, and rookie interaction features.
  • Injury-adjusted regression — Separates injury-driven declines from talent-driven declines; flags bounce-back candidates.
  • Contract signals — Prior-year contract context helps capture role stability, especially in WR/TE experiments.
  • Conformal 80% target ranges — Split-calibrated on 2025 rows; marginal target, not an individual guarantee.
  • ADP matching — Robust via football_name (handles apostrophes, suffixes, nicknames).

Data Sources

  • nflverse (via nfl_data_py) — Seasonal stats, rosters, team stats, OL metrics, snap counts, injuries, depth charts
  • Fantasy Football Calculator — ADP (half-PPR) and bye weeks
  • FantasyPros — K and DEF projections (ML is not reliable for these; we use consensus instead)
  • Pro-Football-Reference — Combine data, draft capital, college production stats
